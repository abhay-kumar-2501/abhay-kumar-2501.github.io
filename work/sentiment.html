§ 01

The question

What we built, and the rules we held it to.

Markets react to headlines. That part isn’t controversial. The useful question is narrower: if you score the tone of the news each day, can you trade on it the next — and beat simply holding the index?

Mohammed Almasbah and I tested it for FIN 465. We scored roughly 6.5 million U.S.-economy news stories (Alexandria Technology’s AE@USA feed, 2000–2023) and lined them up against daily S&P 500 close-to-close returns.

Timing is where studies like this quietly cheat, so we drew a hard line at 4 PM Eastern — timezone- and daylight-saving-aware. A story only counts toward a return it could actually have traded on; after-close, weekend and holiday stories (502,298 of them) roll forward to the next real trading day.

Each day became 15 features across six families: sentiment level, dispersion, volume and attention, probability and confidence, topic-level tone for the Fed and markets, and momentum. Rolling features were shifted so no day could see its own future.

One model did the translating: an Elastic Net. It blends LASSO’s habit of zeroing out weak inputs with Ridge’s steadiness when inputs move together — and fifteen sentiment measures are mostly cousins of each other. We tuned it with GridSearchCV over a five-fold TimeSeriesSplit, trained on 2000 to March 2019, validated on March 2019 to 2023, never shuffled, and fit the scaler on training data only.

Then we locked it and pointed it at two years it had never seen.

Step 1 · Validation, 2019–2023

It dodged COVID. It looked like skill.

The strategy stepped aside during the 2020 crash — then gave the cushion back. It finished at 8.31% a year (Sharpe 0.39) against buy-and-hold’s 13.17% (0.61).

Step 2 · Holdout begins, Jan 2024

Unseen data. At first, it keeps up.

522 trading days the model never saw, and no refitting. Through mid-2024 it tracks the index, and its rolling 60-day hit rate touches about 66%.

Step 3 · The break

Then the streak runs out.

By late 2024 the hit rate falls to about 33%. The April 2025 tariff shock takes the strategy to a −41.08% maximum drawdown; buy-and-hold’s worst was −18.90%.

Step 4 · Verdict

A coin flip, already priced in.

Over the holdout: −12.95% against +47.09%. Information coefficient −0.02 (t = −0.47), directional accuracy 49.81%, Sharpe −0.41 against 1.30.

§ 03

What the model kept

Fifteen features in. Six survived the penalty.

Tuning landed on alpha = 6×10⁻⁵ and an l1_ratio of 0.95 — an Elastic Net that is almost pure LASSO. It kept six of the fifteen features and set the other nine to exactly zero.

The penalty did its job: it kept the model small and honest inside the sample. It couldn’t make the inputs predictive. On the holdout the results are what the efficient-markets view predicts — by the time a story is published, its sentiment is in the price. Daily news sentiment alone is not a basis for trading.

A hot streak in mid-2024 looked like a signal. The rest of the holdout called it noise.

§ 04What we’d do nextFrom the report’s limitations
  1. 01

    Intraday timing

    A daily close-to-close return blurs when news actually lands. Matching stories to intraday prices would test whether any edge lives in the first minutes — before it’s priced.

  2. 02

    Regime-conditional interactions

    Sentiment may matter only in some markets — calm or stressed. Interacting features with the regime would let the model say so, instead of averaging it away.

  3. 03

    Transaction costs

    A daily long/short flip isn’t free. Adding costs would raise the bar a signal has to clear — and this one didn’t clear the bar before costs.

  4. 04

    Sentiment plus price context

    Pair the news with price-based context — realized volatility, the yield curve — so tone is read against the market it lands in, not on its own.
